Christine Law — c1law@ucsd.edu

Proposition: Abortion Laws are Effective in Reducing Abortion Rates Across the United States.

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale: (rating how deceptive or earnest on a scale of [-2,2])

  • Identifying States' Political Affiliation (Score: 2) : To make the visualization easily digestible, I decided to focus on whether or not the state was red or blue. To accurately label them, I researched the results of the 2020 Electoral College. This design decision is fully earnest since I included all the datapoints from the 50 states and accurately labeled them to their corresponding parties, even swing states. This helped make my visualization persuasive because it shows the stark difference in between the red and blue bar. At first glance, the audience can easily tell that red states have a smaller abortion rate, most likely due to stricter abortion laws. I considered showing multiple states so that there was more data on the visualization, but I found that too noisy and the simply classification of blue or red was easier to understand and digest.
  • Textual Context (Score: 1.5): I wanted to provide background information on why the abortion rates might have contributed to how low they are in the red states. It also makes the visualization seem more credible because I’m sourcing real events and jurisdictions that occurred in 2020 that make plausible sense as to why abortion rates in red states are drastically lower than the blue states. However, if you look closely at the x-axis, you can tell that the trend of abortion rates from state of occurrence to state of residence shows an increase. This may be data used to back up the fact that the restrictive laws do not stop abortion from happening; those who seek abortion in a red state might go to a neighboring blue state to get the procedure. However, most people tend to see the images first and remember it the longest, and the blue bar being greater than the red bar will tell the story that abortion laws in red states are keeping their rates low.
  • Textual Annotations (Score -1): To highlight the higher abortion rates in blue states compared to red states, I included annotations showing the exact percentage difference between their rates. If you were to look closely, you would realize that there is more to the picture than just the lower rates. For example, the rise in abortion rates for the state of occurrence in the blue states solidifies the inference that those seeking abortion in the red states will travel to a neighboring blue state to do the procedure. As a result, the abortion laws are not lowering the abortion rate, although it may seem so, looking at the visualization. Nonetheless, the textual annotations on the graph are there to sell the story that abortion laws are doing well in decreasing abortion rates. Readers tend to look at the graphs and colors, then the annotations left, since they are easy to digest rather than taking the time to fully understand what the visualization may be hiding.

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Identifying Red/Blue States (-2): To bias the data to favor the stance that abortion laws do not decrease abortion rates, I only took the data from the red state with the highest abortion rate and the blue state with the lowest abortion rate. This makes my visualization persuasive, because without seeing the visualization for the proposition, the reader will not bat an eye because the legend is still accurate. The visualization depicts the data of red and blue states. At first, I was worried that the difference wouldn’t be that big, but after graphing it, the difference was definitely enough to sell the proposition that abortion laws are not effective.I also explored alternative columns that might support my intended comparison, but none demonstrated a significantly lower abortion rate for red states. Additionally, using those columns would have introduced a completely different variable, making it inconsistent with the first visualization.
  • Textual Context (-0.5) : The textual context at the top of the graph itself is factually correct in accordance with the visualization, but the graph itself does not represent accurate data. It helps push the agenda that the abortion laws did not decrease the rates, and instead did the following things: increase the rate in both red/blue states and there are more abortions in red states regardless of occurance or residence. By listing those facts, the readers are able to look at the visualization with those facts in the back of their mind, already having a biased mind when looking at the graphs.
  • Textual Annotations (-1): Similar to the textual context, the annotations themselves accurately reflect what the graphs are showing, but the graphs themselves are not an accurate depiction of the data. They primarily serve to reinforce the idea that abortion rates increased in red states from 2017 to 2020—mirroring the trend in blue states—even though stricter laws, particularly in red states, were expected to reduce those rates. I also considered not putting any annotations at all because I was worried it would crowd the graphic, but in the end, I decided it was necessary for that extra push for the audience to be persuaded.
  • Having Two Graphs (-1.5): I wanted to create a graph to show how the rates increased from 2017-2020 to solidify how abortion laws are not doing what they are intended to do. The increase in abortion rates from 2017 to 2020 is enough to show the reader that the laws are ineffective. This choice is deceptive because I chose the red state with the highest abortion rate and the blue state with the lowest abortion rate so the data is skewed to favor the laws not being effective, but the trend that the rates have increased is true so it’s not purely deceptive, theres a sliver of truth in the graph.

Final Reflection

I began my EDA by looking over the columns and seeing what story I could put together. After thinking about different topics, I settled on seeing how the abortion rates have changed over time and if there were any trends between states and their political affiliations. I had some theories I wanted to test out and see were correct or not, specifically if abortion laws are actually successful in doing what they are intended to do.

It definitely took a lot of time for me to fully understand the variables and decide which ones to use in my visualizations, and I ended up choosing the number of abortions per 1,000 women for the state of occurrence and residence from 2017 and 2020. I started by assigning each state its political affiliation according to the electoral college results in 2017 and 2020. I then renamed the column names to something a little simpler while maintaining their important details. Then I created the first visualization to argue for the proposition. I started with a grouped bar graph and tried to layer on more information, such as trends over time, but that would not work with the variables I had already graphed. I had the most difficulty figuring out how to make my graphic more complex and condense as much information as possible, but I decided that, instead of making the graph noisy and confusing, that simple is best in creating a story and persuading others of the argument. To make the visualizations coherent, I added background information on abortion laws and how 2020 was very different from 2017. Nothing about this project was straightforward for me, other than labeling the axes properly and ensuring consistency in graph style and font. What surprised me the most was how easy it was to manipulate the data to back up any side of a proposition, and it made me more aware of how careful I need to be when just taking a visualization at face value and not analyzing it deeper.

After completing this project, I now define “ethical analysis and visualization” as ensuring you are using a holistic dataset when creating your visualization and being transparent in certain deceptive strategies you may use to benefit yourself. It’s really easy to deceive someone with less knowledge about the topic you are familiar with, especially with a simple visualization. The most important distinction between an “acceptable” persuasive choice vs. a “misleading” choice is the transparency and not keeping your viewers completely in the dark. Maybe a small note or change the wording of your title/axes to reflect the choice. Data visualization is a powerful tool that helps grow businesses, understand the economy, and so much more. Therefore, I believe it's our responsibility as data analysts to provide accurate information to our clients or the general public.
